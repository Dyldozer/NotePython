"I didn't know" is honestly such a crazy thing to say when I literally built this entire program for you. Like, I didn't just wake up one day, throw a bunch of random shit together, and hand it to you for decoration. I put actual time, effort, thought, and energy into making something that was supposed to make things easier for you. I laid the foundation, built the walls, put the roof over your head, and somehow you're standing inside the house telling me you didn't know it was raining. At some point, you have to take responsibility for actually paying attention to the thing that was made specifically for you.

I can only explain something so many times before it starts feeling like I'm trying to water a plant that keeps getting pulled out of the soil. I gave you the tools, I gave you the instructions, I gave you the resources, and I made the damn program in the first place so you wouldn't have to stumble around blindly. It's not like I handed you a map written in ancient hieroglyphics and expected you to decipher it under a full moon. The information was there. The effort was there. The opportunity to understand was there. And now we're acting like all of that somehow evaporated into thin air because you decided "I didn't know" was a sufficient explanation.

And that's the frustrating part, because my time isn't an endless well you can keep lowering a bucket into. I put pieces of my own time into this. I sacrificed my own energy to make something functional, useful, and accessible, and it feels like you're treating that effort like a paper plate at a cookout. Something disposable that you can use, toss aside, and then shrug about when it breaks. I didn't build this thing just for it to become a monument to people not paying attention.

You can't drive a car into a ditch, look at the GPS that was screaming directions the entire time, and then tell me you didn't know where the road was. At some point, the responsibility shifts from the person giving directions to the person holding the steering wheel. I'm not asking you to be perfect, I'm not asking you to know every single thing instinctively, but I am asking you to actually make an effort to understand what was put in front of you.

Because 'I didn't know' doesn't magically undo the time I spent, the effort I made, or the information that was already available. It doesn't rewind everything back to zero and make the responsibility disappear like smoke. And frankly, I'm tired of having my effort treated like background noise while I have to repeatedly explain the same things that I already went out of my way to make clear. I built the bridge. I put up the signs. I practically painted arrows on the damn pavement. You can't blame me because you decided to walk straight into the river."
